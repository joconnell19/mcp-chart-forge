01 · Why we're doing this

Swedemom's future runs through production.

For months we've been asking everyone to produce more, and more efficiently, because Swedemom only becomes self-sustaining if turning donations into listings gets faster without getting worse. How much good work we finish each hour decides how far the mission goes. Now we want that extra effort to pay you too.

Work efficiently, earn more. Everyone wins.

When you work efficiently and well, you save Swedemom real money, and you should share in it, the way salespeople earn commission. You take home more, more items get listed, and the mission grows. That's the idea behind this production bonus, and we want to build it with you, not hand it to you.

What we promise from the start

Your wage never goes down

Bonuses only add. No penalties on pay, no clawbacks.

Everyone can win

No curve and no ranking. You're measured against the bar, not each other.

A bar that rises only with proof

It goes up when better tools are proven to save time, never because you worked harder.

You help shape it

An anonymous survey first, then a practice run where nothing is paid.

02 · At a glance

Two ways to earn more.

Your paycheck would have up to three parts. The first never changes. The other two are new, and they're pure upside: more earning potential for everyone.

1 · YOUR BONUS

Clear the bar, keep half.

Each item you finish earns "minutes" based on how long that kind of item normally takes. Earn more minutes than you worked, and you've saved Swedemom time.

You keep half of the time you save, paid at your wage. There's no set limit on how much you can earn.

2 · TEAM BONUS

Hit the goal together.

Each stage has a weekly goal. If your stage hits it, everyone in it earns +$0.50 for every hour worked that week.

Beat it by 10% and it doubles to +$1.00 an hour.

Only good work counts. Items sent back for fixes don't earn anything until they're right. The bonus is about doing more good work, not just more work (page 9).

03 · Your bonus, part 1

Every item is worth minutes.

A paperback is quicker to image than a lamp. So instead of counting items, each item earns the minutes it normally takes at that stage. Harder items are worth more, so there's no reason to hunt for the easy bins.

Example imaging values, for illustration only. Real values come from Swedemom's own data and stay fixed once set.

Your pace

Add up the minutes your items earned and compare them with the minutes you worked on production.

minutes earned ÷ minutes worked
= your pace

Different jobs, one score

An imaged item earns imaging minutes; an intake item earns intake minutes. If you help in several stages, it all adds up into one score, so jumping in where you're needed never costs you.

Breaks and other work (truck runs, wrapping glass, meetings) are paid and don't count against you (page 12).

07 · Quality

Only good work counts.

Programs that pay for "more" can backfire if quality slips. Ours only counts items that pass. A send-back lowers that week's bonus, never your wage, and the item comes back to you quickly so you can fix it and learn from it.

Price doesn't change your minutes

An item earns minutes for its category and stage, never for what it's worth. So there's no reason to grab the pricey ones, and a valuable item sent back costs you more than the care it takes to get it right.

Feedback that comes fast

We're building instant checks so problems are caught while the item is still in front of you, not days later (page 13). Your stage page shows what "good" means for your work.

Disagree with a send-back? Dispute it in a tap in the Hub. A lead for your area reviews it, and a mistaken one is reversed before bonuses are paid.

08 · The team bonus

Each stage, one weekly goal.

Managers set a weekly goal for each stage before the week starts. When a stage hits it, everyone who worked in it shares the reward, so helping a teammate pays too.

Example numbers only. Sorting and fulfillment have goals too.

Work in more than one stage?

Your team bonus follows your work. If 60% of your minutes this week came from imaging and 40% from intake, 60% of your team bonus follows imaging's result and 40% follows intake's.

Worked out automatically. Nothing to log.

How goals work

  • Set before the week starts, the way weekly goals are set today.
  • Counted in good items, so you can follow them during the week.
  • Volunteer items count toward the goals. Volunteers don't earn bonuses.

09 · How the bar rises

The bar rises only with proof.

The bar will go up over time. That's how Swedemom grows. But it only goes up for a proven reason, like a new tool that makes the work faster. It never rises just because you got faster. Studies of that trap found output 20 to 40% lower, so the rules are written down before we start.

PROVEN

Only after a measured change

The bar moves only when a new tool or process is measured before and after, on our own floor.

SHARED

By half the gain

If a tool makes the work 20% faster, the bar rises 10%. The other half is easier bonus for you.

ANNOUNCED

Two weeks ahead

Posted two weeks in advance, at most twice a quarter, with the before-and-after numbers.

What you'll see. Just like Production Metrics today: your own numbers, your stage's totals and the team averages. No public leaderboard.

10 · Hours, breaks and payday

Simple to track, clear on your paycheck.

Your hours

  • Clock in and out like you do now. We're testing clocking in right in the Hub, so everything lives in one place.
  • Rest breaks come out automatically. Nothing to log, and they never hurt your pace. Take them.
  • Other work goes on a one-tap timer. Truck runs, wrapping glass, meetings, training, equipment that's down: paid as always, and left out of your pace.
  • Bonus minutes start at your scheduled start time. Attendance itself stays a normal conversation with your manager, not a bonus penalty.
  • Managers look over the time log weekly. Normal ups and downs are fine. It's reviewed, not policed.

Your paycheck

  • Bonuses appear as their own lines on your normal paycheck.
  • The Hub shows the math behind them: items, minutes, the bar and the result.

Bonuses are paid one paycheck later. That gives send-backs, reviews and disputes time to finish, so the number you get is right the first time. A problem found later, like an item in the wrong bin, counts in the week it's found, the way a store counts a return in the month it comes back. It lowers that week's bonus, never your wage or a bonus already paid. If you leave Swedemom, any bonus you've earned is paid in your final check.

Stage guide · Sorting

Sorting decides what's worth it.

Sorters choose what goes into production, so sorting is credited on the value of what you send in, not just how much you handle.

Why we're asking everyone to sort in the Sort tool

We know: eyeballing a box can feel faster than scanning it. But what you decide at sorting shapes everything after it, and the Sort tool is the only way that decision gets recorded.

  • It's how sorting gets credited. Work done outside the tool can't count toward your bonus or your team's goal.
  • It sends the right things to eBay. Price estimates help route each item to where it earns the most.
  • It teaches us what sells. Every scan improves pricing and shows which donations are worth the most.
  • We'll keep making it faster. Tell us what slows you down in it.

What earns credit

The estimated value of items you send into production through the Sort tool, using the system's estimate.

What counts as good

Items that move forward. Things rejected later at intake don't count, so send in what's worth listing.

Your team goal

A weekly goal for items sorted into production through the Sort tool, set by managers.

Stage guide · Intake + description

Intake + description.

Every item you intake and describe earns its standard minutes. The bar starts around 8.6 average items an hour (about 7 minutes each) and is reset on real hours before pay.

What earns minutes

Each item, by category. Harder categories (jewelry lots, coins, detailed collectibles) earn more minutes than quick ones.

What counts as good

Items that pass Final Check: right details, measurements and condition. Flagged items count once they're fixed.

Your team goal

Items intaken and described in the week, set by managers before the week starts.

Get the valuable ones right

Minutes come from an item's category, not its price, so rushing a valuable piece gains you nothing. Getting its details and price right keeps it from coming back to you.

Stage guide · Imaging

Imaging.

Every item you image earns its standard minutes. The bar starts around 15 average items an hour (about 4 minutes each) and is reset on real hours before pay.

What earns minutes

Each item, by category. A paperback earns less than a lamp or a piece of glassware that needs careful shots.

What counts as good

Images that pass the quality check. Items sent back for re-imaging count once they're redone.

Your team goal

Items imaged in the week, set by managers before the week starts.

Feedback while the item is in front of you

We're building a check that runs as photos upload, so problems are caught right away instead of days later. Re-imaging comes back to whoever imaged the item first, so you get the chance to fix your own work.

Stage guide · Put-away

Put-away.

An item can't go live without a bin, so put-away is what turns finished work into listings. Shelving earns minutes like any other stage.

What earns minutes

Each item shelved, with its standard minutes.

What counts as good

The item is in the bin the Hub says it's in, so it can be found and shipped when it sells. An item later found in the wrong bin counts in the week it's found.

Your team goal

Items put away in the week, sized to the people we have on put-away.

More hands on put-away

Put-away depends on very few people today. We'll train more people on it, and anyone who helps shelve earns put-away minutes, so jumping in when carts pile up pays.

Why it matters to everyone. Finished items waiting on a cart can't sell. Same-day put-away is one of the fastest ways to grow sales.

Stage guide · Fulfillment

Fulfillment.

Orders earn minutes just like items do, by type of order. Pack orders well and quickly, and the time you free up for production earns minutes too.

What earns minutes

Each order, by type: a single small item, several items, or a large or fragile piece.

What counts as good

Orders that arrive safely, correct and on time. Damage is reviewed with photos. Carrier damage doesn't count against you, only packing mistakes, in the week the case is settled.

Your team goal

Every order shipped on time and right, every day of the week.

Think an item won't survive shipping? Flag it as a fragile risk in a tap before it ships. A lead decides what to do, and it can't count against you.

Finish strong, then help produce

Order volume depends on what sells, so fulfillment is measured on how well and how efficiently orders go out. Wrap up the day's orders, then jump into production: those minutes count toward the same score.

Other important work, like checking that items are in the right bin and clearing out stale inventory, isn't part of the bonus yet. Tell us in the survey how you'd reward it.

13 · Questions you might have

Frequently asked.

Are these numbers final?

No. This is a draft to get your feedback. Every rule and number will be adjusted with real data before anyone is paid.

Could my pay ever go down?

No. Your hourly wage never changes because of this program. Bonuses only add.

Is this a competition with my coworkers?

No. Everyone is measured against the bar, not each other.

I'm below the bar. What changes for me?

Your pay stays exactly what it is today, and you can still earn the team bonus. Better tools and training will help.

Is there a limit to what I can earn?

No set limit. Unusually big weeks get a quick check that the data is right before payout.

Do I have to rush?

No. Steady, consistent work pays best, and send-backs cost bonus.

Do valuable items earn more?

No. Minutes depend on an item's category and stage, never its price, so there's no reason to compete for the pricey ones. Just get them right.

I do more than one job. How does that work?

Minutes from every stage add up into one score (page 5), and your team bonus follows your mix of work (page 10).

What if equipment breaks or the Hub is down?

If equipment breaks, tap the timer: it's paid and doesn't count against your pace. If the Hub is down, we'll have backup plans.

I'm new. Is that fair to me?

New team members get a fair ramp-up for their first few weeks.

Will the bar go up?

Yes, over time, but only after a new tool or process is proven to save time, by half the gain, with two weeks' notice, at most twice a quarter.

Who sees my numbers?

Your own numbers plus team totals, like Production Metrics today. Managers see individual numbers so they can help.

Why is the bonus a paycheck later?

So reviews and disputes are finished and your number is right the first time. Problems found later count in the week they're found.

What about volunteers?

Volunteer items count toward team goals. Volunteers themselves don't earn bonuses.

Who can I talk to?

Your manager, anytime. Or put it in the survey, where it's anonymous.
